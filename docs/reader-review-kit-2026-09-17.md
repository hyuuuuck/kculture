## Reader tasks

1. **Deoksugung:** You can only arrive after the morning ceremony. Would you still choose this stop? Explain what you could see outside versus inside the palace and one historical detail worth looking for.
2. **Garden Show:** Choose two gardens to compare. What would you look at before reading their descriptions? What can an English-language QR explanation tell you that it cannot guarantee about a guided tour?
3. **OCEAN:** Two adults are choosing between a Korean-heritage experience and a photography exhibition. Who would prefer this exhibition, what is the displayed ticket subtotal, and would September 24 be a valid visit day?
4. **Gwangalli:** You are comparing a September Saturday and an October Saturday. What changes in the show times? Who decides whether that evening's show actually happens?
5. **Andong:** On September 27 at 13:00, can you watch both listed examples as one stop? Choose one place, identify a feature of talchum to watch for, and explain what remains uncertain about tickets.
6. **Jinju:** You intend to see the October 3 opening lighting. Is 18:00 the opening ceremony time? Distinguish the general festival lighting window, the opening ceremony and paid bridges.
7. **First Seoul culture day:** Choose a cultural activity for someone who likes architecture but does not want a language-dependent performance. Explain why a different choice might suit a photography enthusiast, and name a date restriction to check.
8. **Pop-ups:** In the ended BTS example, could someone without a shop reservation enter any part of the site? What would you ask an organizer before relying on a similar future event, and why is a campaign branch list not sufficient?
9. **Weather:** A forecast worsens and your outdoor event is cancelled. Explain why “go indoors” is not a complete backup plan, using the article's September holiday example.
10. **Tax refunds:** One receipt already subtracts the refund; another requires later export confirmation. Explain why the same refund cannot be counted twice, what you need to retain, and what to do before checking the relevant goods into baggage.

For each attempt, record the reader's actual answer, where they looked, any wrong inference, and whether the page let them identify an authoritative next source. A refusal to guess an unconfirmed fact is a useful success, not a failure.

## Editor checklist

- Read all ten articles, not just introductions. Compare important dates, prices, age labels, venue names and exception conditions against their cited sources.
- Check English clarity and cultural interpretation. Editorial observations must not be confused with first-hand attendance or a named tradition not actually on the program.
- Identify material wording that merely restates the source without helping a visitor choose or act. Rewrite or remove it; do not pad it with generic advice.
- Verify sources identified as secondary are not presented as organizer authority. Keep uncertainty next to the claim, not buried only in a footer.
- Check images actually load, captions describe the real assets, and article links work. Basic narrow-screen readability remains to be rechecked; this session's viewport override did not apply.
